# Client Outreach — the "Learn & Earn" step

The task says many interns convert this into a first paid client. This is the exact sequence. Lead with the free work you've already done — that is the whole trick. You are not asking for a favour; you are showing finished output.

---

## Step 1 — WhatsApp / walk-in (do this in person if you can)

> Namaste [Owner name] garu 🙏
>
> I'm Pilli Sandeep, a final-year student and web copywriting intern from [your college / area]. I'm doing a project where I write website copy for local businesses in Vizag.
>
> I picked **[Business name]** because of the reviews — especially people mentioning [specific thing from a review]. So I already wrote the copy for your homepage and services page. It's done, and it's free. No charge, no strings.
>
> Can I show it to you for 5 minutes? If you like it, you can use it. If you don't, just delete it — genuinely no problem.
>
> I can come by [day] evening if that suits you.

**Why this works:** "already written, free, no strings" removes every reason to say no. You're offering finished work, not a proposal.

---

## Step 2 — The 5-minute walkthrough (in person or on a call)

Open the copy deck on your phone. Don't read it out. Point at these four things in this order:

1. **The headline.** "This is what a customer sees first. Right now your site says [their existing line, or nothing]."
2. **The prices.** "Most salons don't publish prices. That's why customers ask the same question 40 times a week. When prices are on the page, Google can send you people searching 'keratin price in Visakhapatnam'."
3. **The fear line.** "This is the line that sells. You tell them when your service is wrong for them, and they trust you for everything else."
4. **The CTA + reply templates.** "And here's what to reply when someone messages — same answer every time, professional, saves you time."

Then stop talking. Let them react.

---

## Step 3 — The offer ladder (never pitch the biggest thing first)

| Tier | What you deliver | Price (suggested for a first client) | Their risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0. Free** | The copy you already wrote (homepage + services) | ₹0 | None |
| **1. Setup** | Put it live: 4-page site on a builder or customise their existing site, add WhatsApp link, Google Business Profile, meta tags | ₹3,000–₹6,000 one-time | Low |
| **2. Content retainer** | Monthly: 2 service updates, 4 Instagram captions, 1 Google post, review-request messages, seasonal offer copy | ₹1,500–₹3,000/month | Low, cancel anytime |
| **3. Full package** | Setup + 3 months retainer + photo/gallery guidance + bridal page | ₹8,000–₹15,000 | Medium — offer only after tier 1 is delivered well |

**What to say when they ask the price:**
> "The copy is yours, free — that part is done. If you want me to actually put it live and set up the WhatsApp booking so enquiries come to you properly, that's ₹[X] one-time. And if you want me to keep it updated every month — offers, festive posts, new services — that's ₹[Y] a month. You can stop any time; it's month to month."

## Step 5 — Objection handling (word-for-word)

**"We already have a website."**
> "Perfect — then this is an upgrade, not a rebuild. I can hand the copy to your existing developer, or update the pages myself. Nothing gets lost."

**"It's too expensive."**
> "Start with tier 1 only. And to be honest, one extra bridal booking covers it several times over."

**"I don't have the time for this."**
> "That's exactly why I've already written it. You need 10 minutes: approve the prices and send me 3 reviews."

**"Can you guarantee customers?"**
> "No, and be careful with anyone who does. What I can do is make sure that when someone *is* looking for your service in this area, they find you, understand you in 5 seconds, and know how to book you. That's the part that's missing today."

**"My nephew can do it for free."**
> "He can, and that's fine. The copy document is free either way. If he wants the prompt system I used, I'll send it to him."

## Step 6 — Review-collection script (send this to your client — it's part of the deliverable)

Send 24 hours after a service, to every happy customer:

> Hi [name], thank you for coming in today! If you're happy with how it turned out, would you mind leaving a quick Google review? It genuinely helps a small studio like ours get found. Here's the link: [review link]. Takes 30 seconds — and if anything wasn't right, just reply here and I'll sort it out personally.

Ask for 1 review a day for a week and they'll get 5–7. Five good reviews with the right keywords ("keratin", "MVP Colony", "bridal makeup") move local search rankings more than any technical SEO tweak.

---

## Step 7 — What to do with the result (either way)

**If they say yes:** deliver tier 1 within 7 days. Then send a short "here's what changed" note with 3 measurable outcomes you can point to (prices visible to Google, WhatsApp booking live, 4 reply templates in use). Ask for a testimonial and one referral — that referral is usually worth more than the fee.

**If they say no:** you still have (a) a finished portfolio piece, (b) permission to show it to other businesses in a different area, and (c) a LinkedIn post. Send the same message to the next business on your list — a dry cleaner, a bakery, a physiotherapy clinic. The system is reusable; only the brief changes.

**Post it on LinkedIn either way.** "Here's the copy I wrote for a local salon — free — and here's why" *is content*. A week later, business owners in your network will start messaging you.
